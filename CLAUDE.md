# NHI-timeline

An interactive timeline of the stories told about non-human intelligence (NHI) and disclosure: which story is told about which era and which place, by whom, in which podcasts and documents, and how well each story is corroborated across the whole corpus. John's project (8 October 2026); it began inside `robot-release` (the Wild Animal album, where the dark side of the moon and the end of *Abend* touch the theme) and is its own repo because the material is larger than a song.

The timeline shows stories, not facts. A story is what someone said happened; the project records who said it, where, when, and who else says the same, and lets the corroboration do the ranking. Nothing here asserts that any story is true.

## Principles

- **Every story carries its tellings.** A story exists in the data only with at least one telling: an episode or document, a time code or page, and the quoted words. A story without a quote is a note, not a story.
- **Corroboration is counted, never judged by taste.** The score is a function of the tellings (how many independent tellers, of what kind, with what documents), normalised against the corpus, so a story's place on the timeline follows from the data and changes when the corpus grows.
- **Independence is explicit.** A teller who repeats another teller is not a second source; the data says whom a teller cites. The same person telling the same story on three podcasts is one teller.
- **Plain files are the database.** JSON for data, Markdown for notes, one file per thing, hand-correctable, diffable. No server, no binary store.
- **Fail loud.** Malformed data or a violated precondition raises with a clear message. A crash beats a quiet wrong score.
- **Living docs.** This file and the others describe the current state; git is the history. No changelogs, no dated narration in docs. No em dashes anywhere; commas, colons, periods, parentheses.
- **Photographs are free or credited, and a face stays beside its own words.** A story's pictures are objects and places (the place at the time preferred, the place today the last resort), or a person picture: a free-licensed photograph of someone the story is about, allowed only when two or more independent tellers tell the story or the person shown tells it themselves, so a face never stands beside one other person's claim about them. Pictures are free-licensed (public domain, CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA, US government works) or credited fair use (news photos, book covers, press images, never a person picture) kept at thumbnail size and flagged `fair-use`. A person's headshot is free-licensed only, never fair use, and the page shows it only beside that person's own tellings, never as a story's picture. While any fair-use image is in `site/images/`, the page stays private until those images are cleared.
- **Sources stay reachable.** Transcripts are pulled from YouTube's own captions (`yt-dlp`), kept under `sources/` by video id, and cited by time code. Nothing is scraped that the platform does not publish. When YouTube will not serve the captions (a rate limit, no caption track), the fallback is creator-watch's Gemini transcript of the video by URL, stored as the episode's caption text and marked `captions_source: gemini` with approximate time stamps.

## Layout

- `data/stories/<slug>.json`: one story each (`schema.md` gives the shape), each with its `plain` telling for a reader new to the subject once `explain.py` has run, `plain_by: "hand"` when a person wrote it. The heart of the project.
- A story may carry `discussion`: the back and forth about it (`debunk`, `rebunk` and `note` entries, each with who says it, the words in the reported voice, and a source URL or an episode and time code), written by hand, kept by merge and link, appended to the survivor on a fold, passed through by `build.py`, validated by `check.py`; `schema.md` gives the shape. Recording a debunk asserts nothing about who is right.
- `data/notes/<story id>.json`: the inbox of viewer notes on a story (`by`, `date`, `text`, `status` open, folded or dropped), pulled by hand from the page's artifact database and folded into the story (usually as a `discussion` entry) by a person; `check.py` validates them, `build.py` counts the open ones as the story's `notes_open`, nothing else reads them.
- `data/people/<slug>.json`: the tellers: name, role (witness, participant, official, researcher, journalist, host), what they cite, public links, and `aliases`, the ids folded into them.
- `data/episodes/<video id>.json`: one podcast episode or interview: show, host, guests, date, URL, duration, the caption file it was read from.
- `data/documents/<slug>.json`: public documents (reports, hearings, articles, FOIA releases): title, date, URL, what it attests.
- `data/eras.json`, `data/places.json`: the timeline's axes, hand-edited; a story names an era by id and a place by id, or gives its own years and coordinates. `data/place-aliases.json` maps the free text the extractor gives for a place to a place id; a story whose text resolves to nothing keeps it as `place_text` and the page groups such stories by it under "Unplaced". `data/person-aliases.json` does the same for people: a spelling the captions or the model give ("Garry Nolan") to the person id it belongs to ("gary-nolan"), read by `person_id`. `data/person-folds.json` is the list of decided folds: each group names the ids of one person under several caption spellings (`members`), the id they become (`canonical`), the name the page shows and the evidence, hand-correctable, applied by `people.py`.
- `data/images/<owner>/<n>.json`: one sidecar per photograph (kind, caption, source page, image URL, licence as stated, `licence_kind` free or fair-use, credit, date, fetched), the owner a story id, an alias of one or a person id; `schema.md` gives the shape. `site/images/<owner>/<n>.jpg` (or `.png` for a source with transparency): the photographs themselves, free ones at most 1200 px on the long side, fair-use ones at most 480 px, git-tracked like the captions; the page loads them by the relative path `images/...`.
- `sources/captions/<video id>.<lang>.vtt` and `.txt`: the captions as fetched and as plain text with time codes (git-tracked: they are the evidence; a few MB each at most).
- `data/candidates/<video id>.json`: what `extract.py` pulled from an episode, before merging (generated; `schema.md` gives the shape). `data/clips.json`: the ledger of the passages two or more episodes share word for word (a clip a host cut in, a sponsor read): each group's occurrences, the verdict, the speaker, the other voices, the origin episode or `outside`, the reason and the model, hand-correctable with `"by": "hand"` (`schema.md` gives the shape). `data/scores.json`: the score summary (the weights, the institutional-trust knob, the median floor and median, the tier counts, the counts, and every story's attestation, score, tier and profile), generated.
- `pipeline/`: `captions.py` (yt-dlp captions in, text with time codes out, entities unescaped, the `>>` speaker change kept as a line prefix; the host and guests filled from the channel and the title), `gemini_captions.py` (the fallback when YouTube will not serve captions: creator-watch's Gemini transcript, run with `../creator-watch/.venv/bin/python`, written as the caption text with `captions_source: gemini`; its own 2 h per UTC day ledger in `sources/gemini-ledger.json`, since the Google free tier is shared with creator-watch), `extract.py` (an episode's text in chunks of about 12,000 words to story candidates through `claude -p` with `prompts/extract.md`, Opus; a quote that is not verbatim is dropped with its reason kept, where `>>` markers and bracketed tags are ignored on both sides and a quote that occurs twice is located nearest the model's time code), `link.py` (each candidate is the `same` story as one already held, a `variant` of one, or `new`, decided through `claude -p` with `prompts/link.md` in groups of about ten candidates per call (`--batch`, the union of their nearest stories offered, a later candidate free to name an earlier one of its group as `#n`; a refused answer is asked once more with a note, then stops the run; the candidate file is saved after each group, so a killed run resumes at the first unlinked candidate) and written into the candidate as `link`, with the grain, the reason and the difference kept; a `same` at another grain, or joining a `program` to another kind, is downgraded to `new`; a `new` or `variant` candidate is a provisional story at once, so later candidates of the same run can join it; a joined telling keeps its own framing as `as_told`; `--existing` folds duplicate stories into the earlier one, one story per call, and keeps the old id in `aliases`; `data/links.json` is the ledger of those decisions and of the downgrades), `merge.py` (candidates into stories, people and documents following `link`, names normalised to one id per person through `data/person-aliases.json`, places resolved through the aliases, with the `same-event` links; takes episode ids to merge one episode at a time), `clips.py` (the clips a host cut into another episode: word 8-gram runs of 15 or more words shared between episodes' captions, grouped so a clip aired three times is one group, judged through `claude -p` with `prompts/clips.md` about six groups a call from each occurrence's episode, guests, date and a minute of captions either side (the publish date never decides the origin), a refused answer asked once more with a note, then the run stops, the ledger `data/clips.json` saved after each call; then a telling inside a replayed occurrence gets `replay_of` and the clip's speaker as `teller`, the extractor's teller kept as `teller_was`; `--no-apply` and `--report` write no story; runs after merge, before score), `people.py` (`--fold` applies `data/person-folds.json`: every member id in the tellings, cites, clips ledger, episodes and image sidecars becomes the canonical id, a member's headshots move under it, the person files merge into the canonical's with the members kept as its `aliases`, the members' names are added to `data/person-aliases.json`, every group checked before anything is written; idempotent; runs after clips, before score), `score.py` (the evidence profile, attestation, score and tier, `scoring.md` is its specification), `explain.py` (a plain telling for every story, written ground up for a reader new to the subject: the stories to `claude -p` with `prompts/explain.md` in groups of about twelve, well-attested first, then told-by-several, then told-once, each by score; the answer must name every id of the group once, a refused answer is asked once more with a note, then stops the run; every story of a group is saved as soon as it is answered, so a killed run resumes; a story with `plain` is skipped unless `--force`, one with `plain_by: "hand"` never touched; `--fixture FILE` gives prepared answers; runs after score, before build), `images.py` (a manifest of found photographs in: every entry checked before any download, an owner that is no story, alias or person, a kind that does not fit it or a fair-use headshot refused, an image URL the owner holds kept, the rest downloaded with urllib, resized and converted with macOS `sips`, written as file and sidecar under the next free number; `--keep-going` lists failed downloads at the end and exits 1; it also holds the sidecar checks and blocks `check.py` and `build.py` use), `build.py` (data to `site/data.json` and `site/data.js`, and each story's `plain` telling to `site/plain.json` and `site/plain.js` (an object of story id to text, beside the data file whatever its name), all written compact so each file stays under the 16 MB a published artifact allows a file, a story in the data file without its `plain`, a telling without the episode's show, title and date (the `episodes` map has them) or the link's `link_reason` and `as_told`, a story without its `related` list (the `links` list has every relation once), with each story's `images` and each person's headshot `image`), `check.py` (every story has a telling, every id resolves, every quote is a verbatim substring of its caption text, every discussion entry, viewer note, image sidecar and the clips ledger is well formed, every `replay_of` is what the ledger makes of its telling, every file under `site/images/` has a sidecar and no folded member id is left anywhere; it then lists, as warnings that never fail it, person ids within two letters of each other, usually one person under two spellings, to fold through `data/person-folds.json`; `--fixture` runs the whole chain offline on `tests/fixtures/`, checks the result against `tests/fixtures/expect.json` and proves merge and link idempotent). `llm.py` is the one wrapper around `claude -p` (isolated from the user's Claude Code setup, structured output by schema); `common.py` holds the data root, the caption text and the slug, year and place parsing. Every stage takes `--root DIR`.
- `tests/fixtures/`: six tiny fictional episodes (captions, episode files, prepared `answers/` instead of a model call, prepared `links/` answers for `link`, `clips.json` answers for `clips`, one hand-written story and document, `data/person-aliases.json`) and `expect.json` (what the chain must produce: time codes, absent people, every story's tier, flag and attestation, the fold, the downgrades, the replayed clip, the person fold) so the chain runs end to end offline. `tests/fixtures/README.md` says what each case proves. Nothing in them is a real claim.
- `prompts/`: the prompts the pipeline sends, as files, never inline strings.
- `site/`: the timeline page in 3D, plain HTML, CSS and JS with three.js 0.170.0 from jsDelivr (the one CDN), no build step: `index.html`, `style.css`, `app.js` (the controller: data, the institutional-trust knob, filters, rails, search, keys, the hash) and `js/` (`model.js` the data check, the score recomputed from each profile at the reader's knob and the tiers; `cards.js` the picture cards both views share (a story's pictures sorted place-then, object, place-now and cut to three, two or one by tier, each loaded lazily into a canvas texture with a shared cache, a picture sprite in a frame of the kind colour); `corridor.js` the default view, flying through density-warped time with the tiers as rings, where a story with pictures shows them as cards in place of its sphere (a well-attested story fans up to three), the sphere a dot at the card's foot and the label under the card; `globe.js` regions as patches and eras as shells, one card above a story's marker; `panel.js` the story panel with the plain telling first, the insider summary under "In the corpus", the story's photographs under it, a teller's headshot beside their own tellings, and Back and forth; `notes.js` the notes box, saved to the artifact's `notes` collection when published with the `db` capability, copied as text elsewhere). Served over http it reads `site/data.json`; opened from disk (`file:`) it reads `site/data.js`, the same object, because a page from disk cannot fetch; `?data=<relative path>` loads another copy from beside the page. After the first draw it loads the plain tellings the same way, `plain.json` over http and `plain.js` from disk or for a `.js` data file, from the data file's folder; until they arrive the panel shows the summary alone. `#story=<id or alias>`, `mode=globe`, `rail=`, `trust=` set the start. On a coarse pointer (a phone or tablet), or with `#touch` on a computer, the page shows a touch pad of held keys and touch guidance instead of the keys list: one finger looks around, two fingers move along time in the corridor and pinch the globe. At load it checks its recompute against the stored attestation and score and refuses to draw when they differ. Published as a private artifact (with `db`) or opened locally.
- `notes/`: research notes: the seed timeline (from `robot-release/research/nhi-disclosure-narratives.md`), the queue, `prior-art.md` (the timelines and databases that exist and what this project adds), `trust-prior.md` (the research behind the weights and the institutional-trust knob).
- `schema.md`: the data shapes. `scoring.md`: how the score is computed and why.

## The score, in short

Each story carries an evidence profile: its independent tellers by kind, its documents, its repetitions and a `contradicted` flag, where two tellers are independent only if neither cites the other and they are not the same person, and a replayed clip is its speaker's telling, never the episode guest's. Attestation weighs the profile (first-hand witness or participant 1.0, official 0.8 times an institutional-trust knob from 0.5 to 1.25 that the page can move, journalist citing named sources 0.6, second-hand 0.5, researcher's summary 0.25, host 0.1; a document 0.5 when it is itself a telling and 0.25 when only credited; `notes/trust-prior.md` grounds the values). The corroboration score is the attestation divided by the corpus median attestation, so 1.0 means "as well attested as the typical story here" and the scale moves as the corpus grows; the tier (`well-attested`, `told-by-several`, `told-once`) names the band the page foregrounds or backseats. A contradiction flags and never reduces. Two stories overlap when they share an event or a document, or when `merge.py` or a person has marked them `same-event`, `variant`, `corroborates` or `contradicts`; the page draws overlaps as links and `contradicts` in a different colour. A shared teller is a count on each story ("also told by this teller: N stories"), never a line. `scoring.md` holds the full rule and its open questions.

## Working here

- Python 3.12, `uv`, no dependencies beyond the standard library; `../creator-watch` checked out beside this repo, with its `.venv` and keys file, for the Gemini fallback only; `yt-dlp` on the path; `claude` on the path for the extraction pass. `uv run python -m pipeline.<stage>`; the order is in `README.md` under Run. `uv run python -m pipeline.check --fixture` after any change to the pipeline.
- A story is edited by hand in its JSON when the pipeline gets it wrong; `check.py` keeps hand edits honest.
- Quotes are verbatim from the captions, with the video id and the time code: a substring of the caption text with the `[h:mm:ss]` prefixes stripped and whitespace collapsed, case and punctuation exact. A paraphrase is marked as one.
- Git: commit after each piece of work; `origin` is `git@github-personal:John-Therrien-WA/NHI-timeline.git`; push at the end of a session; never force-push.
- Prior art and the hazard list (what must never be stated as fact, the platforms' labelling rules) live in `notes/` and are read before anything is published.
