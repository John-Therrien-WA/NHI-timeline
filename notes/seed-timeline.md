# Non-human intelligence and the disclosure narratives

Background for the NHI strain John wants in the release (his words, 8 Oct 2026: "Abend is about it pretty directly at the end, and for me the dark side of the moon is a place where NHIs are, it's also a way to exchange coherence with others in a way that has other influences than direct sun contact"). "Undated" means the page shows no date. **SNIPPET** means the claim rests on a search-engine summary, not a full page read. **UNVERIFIED** means a source was blocked, conflicting, or missing. Band copy is quoted from `BRIEF.md` exactly. Nothing here is a finding about whether non-human intelligence exists; it is a map of what people say, when they said it, and what is documented.

## 1. Summary

1. Jesse Michels is the host of *American Alchemy*, a weekly YouTube interview show (channel `@JesseMichels`, about 740,000 subscribers) that since 2021 has become one of the main stages for the disclosure narrative. Of its roughly 190 public videos, about 120 concern UFOs, non-human intelligence (NHI) or disclosure; 47 are listed below with dates and guests. Michels talks to everyone and says so ("I've definitely gotten things wrong"); the show is pro-inquiry, not neutral.
2. The public record is thin and the narrative is thick. What is documented: the Pentagon studied the subject (2017), the intelligence community cannot explain most of its own reports (2021 onward), a former intelligence officer told Congress under oath that he was told of crash retrievals and "nonhuman" biologics (2023), the Senate majority leader wrote "non-human intelligence" into draft law (2023), and the government's own office found no evidence of any of it (2024). The narratives grow in the gap between those sentences.
3. There are nine distinct narratives, not one: crash retrieval and reverse engineering; the control system (Vallée); angels and demons (Pasulka, Nell); consciousness first, the "headset" (Hoffman); it is already among us (Nolan, Strieber); psionics, the summoned craft (Barber); the Moon as artificial or inhabited (Carlson, Derakhshani); controlled disclosure on a schedule (Nell); and the counter-narrative, disinformation and limited hangouts (AARO, the Wall Street Journal, and Michels himself on Rogan). A song can borrow the shapes without picking a winner.
4. The far side of the Moon as a place of non-human presence is folklore all the way down: a 1969 hoax by a science-fiction writer, a 1976 book of smudged photographs, a psychic's 1998 memoir, a 2001 press-conference testimony, a 2007 April Fools' video, and, in 2025 and 2026, Michels' own two Moon episodes, one of which he opens by calling "stepwise more speculative than any conversation you've heard". The documented facts are stranger and better for a song: the far side is not dark, it was unseen until 1959, every Apollo crew spent about 45 minutes of each orbit in radio silence behind it, and since 2018 the only way to talk to anything there is through a relay satellite parked at a point where the Moon blocks the Earth.
5. "Contact as something other than sunlight" has a real physics: earthshine (sunlight twice reflected), moonbounce (amateur radio bounced off the Moon since 1953), laser ranging off mirrors Apollo left in 1969, and the Queqiao relays. Hoffman and Michels reach the same image from the other side: "we're windows and the sunlight peering through the windows is what's ultimately binding all of us" (Michels, 2026-06-02, 02:48:56).
6. The band's lyrics already carry the strain: Soulstice's "Winter flowers bloom / On the dark side of the moon / No need to pray sunshine", Abend's "Tiny blue where tall tales tune the truth / A distance so vast / Space time became / A thing of the past", and Gödel's "Sing a song to be seen / On liquid crystal TV". None asserts contact; all are usable as they stand.
7. The hazard is not the platforms, which allow this (a clearly animated robot telling a story needs no label), but the frame: the account must never state as fact that NHI exist, that craft or bodies were recovered, or that anything is on the Moon, and it must never put a real person's face or voice next to a claim they did not make. Everything is "the robot says", "the file says", or a sourced quote in brackets.

### 4a. The far side of the Moon as a place of non-human presence

**The documented facts first** (all FACT):

- The far side is not dark. The Moon rotates once per 27.3-day orbit, so one hemisphere always faces Earth; both sides get about fourteen Earth days of sunlight a month, and at new Moon the far side is fully lit. "Dark" meant unseen (PBS NewsHour quoting John Keller, NASA LRO, https://pbs.org/newshour/science/never-see-far-side-moon, undated; Sky at Night, https://www.skyatnightmagazine.com/space-science/dark-side-of-the-moon, undated).
- Unseen until 1959-10-07, when Luna 3 photographed it from 63,500 km (NASA NSSDC, https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/imgcat/html/object_page/lu3_phc6.html). First seen by human eyes on 1968-12-24, Apollo 8, during a loss of signal of "32 minutes and 37 seconds" (NASA, https://www.nasa.gov/history/50-years-ago-apollo-8-in-lunar-orbit/).
- Every Apollo lunar orbit of about two hours had roughly 45 to 47 minutes of radio silence behind the Moon (NASA Apollo Flight Journal, Apollo 12, 14 and 17, https://www.nasa.gov/history/afj/ap12fj/19day7_rev36_43.html, https://www.nasa.gov/history/afj/ap14fj/15_day4_descent_orbit_insertion.html, https://www.nasa.gov/history/afj/ap17fj/13_day05_part3_doi.html). Almost half of every orbit was out of contact.

Two people who cannot see each other, talking by way of the Moon.

## 7. Hazards

**What the account must never state as fact** (CLAUDE.md: nothing false about who made the music; the robot "may claim, doubt and deny a mind, all inside the story"; the same line holds here):

1. That non-human intelligence exists, has visited, or is "already here". Every such sentence is a quote with a name, a date and a source, or the robot's line inside the story.
2. That craft or bodies were recovered, that a program exists, or that any document proves it. The documented position of the US government on 2026-10-08 is "no verifiable evidence" (Kosloski, 2024-11-19; AARO, 2024-03-08 and 2026-07-21).
3. That anything is on the Moon. The far side is photographed, landed on and sampled; every presence claim is folklore (4a). A caption may say "[the robot believes someone is there]"; it may not say "there is a base".
4. That a real, named person said something they did not say. Grusch, Elizondo, Nolan, Nell, Pasulka, Vallée, Hoffman, Levin, Michels, Carlson, Derakhshani and Wolfe are living or recently dead people with exact words on record; quote them with the time code or not at all. Wolfe died in 2018; Vallée was born in 1939.
5. That the band, or the robot, has inside knowledge. Michels' own line applies: "I don't know if there's a saucer in a hangar at Wright-Patt."
6. That the Apollo recordings say more than they say. The 2026-06-12 "alien star base" line is one voice on a science debrief, possibly a joke.

**What the labelling rules imply** (research/platform-rules.md, as of 2026-10-01):

- A clearly animated robot telling a story about the Moon is not "realistic-looking" content, so TikTok's and Meta's AI labels are optional; any C2PA-carrying asset gets labelled anyway, and stripping metadata breaks TikTok's terms. Nothing changes here.
- TikTok bans content "misleading about matters of public importance" even when labelled (guidelines effective 2026-09-24). A post that reads as "the Pentagon confirmed aliens" or "NASA hides a base" is in that zone; a post that reads as a robot's wish is not. Keep the claim inside the character and the quotes in brackets.
- Real people's faces or synthesized voices next to the robot's claims trip TikTok's fake-endorsement rule and, in the EU, the deep-fake clause of AI Act Art. 50(4) (from 2026-08-02). A reconstructed Armstrong, Aldrin or Grusch voice is out. Their documented words, as text captions with the source, are not. NASA mission audio (the Apollo loss-of-signal exchanges) is US government work and generally usable, but check NASA's media-usage guidelines before a release that uses it.
- The AI-provenance line the account carries from the first post ("agents make it") is also the hazard's cure: a story told by machines about non-human minds is self-evidently a story.
